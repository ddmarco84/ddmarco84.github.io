Research

My research examines how incentives and connections between people, firms and countries shape economic outcomes.

Working Papers

Incentive Design on Networks (with A. Daripa and S. Kapur)

How can incentives encourage people to form a network that benefits society when their productivity is private information?

We design a mechanism for network formation with privately known productivities that implements the socially optimal network through transfers based on opportunity costs, ensuring ex post incentive compatibility, pairwise stability, and budget balance.

Read working paper on SSRN

Network Structures and Credit Rating (with A. Daripa and S. Kapur)

Can the connections agents choose reveal information useful for credit ratings?

We study whether productivity-consistent credit ratings can be inferred from endogenous network structure, showing that under suitable link costs equilibrium networks separate types and sustain ratings aligned with productivity.

Read working paper on SSRN

Journal article

Decentralized Defence of a (Directed) Network Structure, Defence and Peace Economics, 2020.

When do individual decisions about security protect a network as well as coordinated decisions?

I analyse how agents in a directed network allocate defence resources under external threats, showing that decentralised choices can be efficient with a strategic attacker but generally diverge from the social optimum under random attacks.

Journal article

Centrality in Trade Networks and Investment in Security (with V. Bove and L. Elia), Peace Economics, Peace Science and Public Policy, 2016.

How does a country’s position in global trade networks relate to its military spending?

We show that a country’s position in global trade networks—measured by network centrality—systematically shapes and constrains its military spending decisions.
